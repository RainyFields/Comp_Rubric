\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The central result is unchanged in direction and cleaner in evidence: on intact contexts, RL on
\bcplus{} lifts an 8B agent by 16--17 points at temperature 1.0 within 46--59 reward-bearing
steps, the folding mechanism is learned, and \foldgrpo{} and \grpo{} remain statistically
indistinguishable. Read together with the earlier study, two independently trained pairs of
arms, one on corrupted and one on intact observations, now agree that the paper's
\foldgrpo{}-over-\grpo{} margin does not manifest at 8B under a matched protocol. This
independently confirms the earlier verdict rather than extending it; the paper's 36B result
may hold at that scale, and our design does not test it.

The behavioral findings qualify two claims of the earlier report. Empty reasoning under RL is
a reward effect at 8B and not a format artifact: the bug explained the base model's empty-think
rate, which the fix halved, but the trained policies re-learned the behavior on intact
contexts within 10--20 steps. A plausible reading is that, for a binary outcome reward on
multi-hop browsing, visible reasoning consumes response budget without changing which tool
call comes next, so trajectories that skip it are shorter, finish more often, and are rewarded
more often. \foldgrpo{}'s process penalties on unfolded tokens would strengthen that pressure,
which is consistent with its higher rate. A rival explanation, that the judge or the branch
summaries reward brevity directly, is not excluded by our data. The second qualification is
that \foldgrpo{}'s context-management advantage does not survive intact observations. The
mechanism still emerges, but the earlier six-fold reduction in blow-out rate was measured on
trajectories whose observations had been shortened by the bug, and on full observations
\foldgrpo{}'s branch-heavy trajectories overflow the 32k budget more often than \grpo{}'s.

The methodological lesson is about evaluation validity for agent RL rather than about folding.
Template-diff tokenization is a natural implementation for any chat template, and it fails
silently for any template that is not prefix-stable; the Qwen3 family is one such case. The
corrupted policies produced plausible scores and coherent text, and the defect was found only
by checking whether each observation in a dump carried its role header. We recommend that
agent-RL codebases assert exact token equality between the sliced turn and a direct encode of
the same message, as our tests now do, and that validation dumps be audited for observation
integrity before any cross-arm comparison is reported. The earlier report's finding that folded
policies collapse under chat-templated serving was itself a symptom of this bug, since
re-templating on the server restored the headers that training had never shown the policy.

The main boundary of the present comparison is its budget. \foldgrpo{} received 46 and \grpo{}
59 reward-bearing updates of 100 planned, so the arms are compared at a matched 40 steps and
at their last uncontaminated checkpoints rather than at convergence. The step-40 comparison
is the cleaner test and shows no difference; the mild sample-efficiency hint at the final
checkpoints (equal temperature-1.0 score with 13 fewer updates) would need the resumed runs to
confirm. The single seed per arm, the 8B scale, and the substituted judge bound the claim in
the same way as before. The discriminating next experiment is a resumption of both arms from
their last clean checkpoints to 100 reward-bearing steps, with the reward server co-located in
each training job so that queue reclamation cannot interrupt reward delivery; if the tie
persists at convergence, the 36B margin becomes the natural next test.
